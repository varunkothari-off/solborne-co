Know who needs a reminder—and who needs you.

When work is complete and invoices are outstanding, keep routine follow-ups moving and bring the right conversations to your attention.

  1. Invoice due
  2. Check payment and dispute status
  3. Approved follow-up
  4. A person resolves exceptions
  5. Record the outcome

Routine work

Payment checks, approved reminder rules, payment links and a clear next action. Stop reminders when payment is verified.

When a person steps in

Disputes, broken promises and relationship-sensitive conversations need judgement. Bring the invoice history, response and reason for attention together.

What outcomes can teach us

Record what followed each reminder or conversation, including unresolved cases. Evaluate timing and prioritisation before claiming any improvement.

Paid after contact does not prove paid because of contact. We do not guarantee collection or publish unvalidated bad-debt predictions.

Get client work ready.

Know which clients need a nudge—and which need help.

When work depends on information from someone else, know exactly what is missing and what is ready for professional review.

  1. Agree the checklist
  2. Receive the materials
  3. Check missing items
  4. A person resolves uncertainty
  5. Confirm readiness

Routine work

Clear requests, an easy submission route, specific missing-item follow-ups and a shared readiness record. Use existing tools where they fit.

When a person steps in

Confusing requests, sensitive materials, repeated wrong submissions or deadline exceptions need a person. Provide the missing item, prior attempts and decision needed.

What outcomes can teach us

Learn which requests produce complete, usable submissions and which items cause rework. Measure accepted completeness, not upload counts.

An upload is not proof a document is correct. A professional confirms whether the information is sufficient. Never send passwords or unredacted customer records into the Walk.

Prepare a sound quotation.

Know which quotes are ready to approve—and which need your judgement.

When enquiries arrive while you are doing paid work, capture the requirement once and prepare a draft that respects your pricing and scope.

  1. Capture the requirement
  2. Ask for missing details
  3. Prepare the draft
  4. Owner approves scope and price
  5. Record decision and job result

Routine work

Structured intake, missing-detail checks, calculations from approved pricing and draft assembly. An accepted quote can hand off to the next agreed step.

When a person steps in

Unusual scope, feasibility, margin exceptions and negotiation need your judgement. Show the assumptions and uncertain items before any commitment.

What outcomes can teach us

Compare draft corrections, customer decisions and actual delivery effort. Improve estimates without treating acceptance alone as a profitable outcome.

Complex estimating still needs expertise. We do not promise that faster quotes win work or change your prices without approval.
